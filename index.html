Nice to meet you, I’m Harsh

I’m a Computer Science student in Dehradun, India, and I spend most of my time inside Kubernetes and the open-source projects built around it. Almost everything I do starts with one question: when a system says something worked, did it actually?

Over the last few months that question has turned into fixes in Kyverno, PipeCD, KEDA and Volcano, plus a lot of reading and reviewing other people’s pull requests. The bug I keep running into is the same one wearing different clothes: the dashboard is green, the status says Synced, and something underneath is broken. Before open source, I interned at IBM, where I built a multilingual disaster-response assistant, and at SafePay, where I triaged security alerts and ran vulnerability scans. The maintainers I work with are spread across time zones, so most of it happens in writing, which is why I attach the proof to everything I say.

I’m also in my final year of a B.Tech in Computer Science (Cybersecurity and Forensics) at UPES Dehradun. Forensics is really just figuring out what happened from what was left behind, and that’s how I approach software too: read what it says, then go look at the logs, the config and the live state, and write down where they don’t match. If I can’t reproduce something, I don’t post it.
